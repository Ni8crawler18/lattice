RTO here runs 15–30% against 2–5% globally, and over 45% traces to bad addresses. That is shipping paid twice for revenue booked zero.

Why software can't fix it:

𝗔𝗻 𝗜𝗻𝗱𝗶𝗮𝗻 𝗮𝗱𝗱𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗶𝘀 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝗮𝗻 𝗮𝗱𝗱𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘀. 𝗜𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝗱𝗶𝗿𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀.

"Ganesh mandir ke peeche, blue gate wala ghar, opp SBI ATM, Kothrud"

No street number. A temple as the reference point. Hinglish today, Devanagari tomorrow. Every system assumes house → street → postcode, so it doesn't fail loudly — it returns a confident pin 800 metres off.

What exists today:

→ Shipping platforms like Shiprocket optimise routes. They consume address quality, they don't produce it.
→ DIGIPIN (India Post + ISRO) codes every 4m x 4m cell — but encodes coordinates, not text.
→ DHRUVA, the Address-as-a-Service framework, is the rails going on top.

The state is building the destination. But hundreds of millions of legacy addresses in CRMs and loan files have no coordinates, and nobody is solving the actual gap: reading the language the address is written in.

This fails at the language layer, not the formatting layer. That's why regex and English-first geocoders never cracked it — and why Indic-native models finally can.

𝗧𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝗟𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗲 — Indian Address Intelligence, built on Sarvam.

• Parse — any script into clean fields, landmarks kept as first-class data with their spatial relation (behind / opposite / near)
• Resolve — are these two differently-written records the same physical door? Validation APIs check one address in isolation. Nobody answers this.
• Score — delivery risk before dispatch, plus the one question to ask the customer

lattice01 / Problem

The problem

Indian addresses are not addresses.
They are directions.

Landmarks instead of street identity, four scripts, no canonical spelling, one door written a dozen ways. Software built for house → street → postcode doesn't fail loudly here — it returns a confident answer that is quietly wrong.

Silent failure is the expensive kind — nobody knows to fix it.

lattice03 / The insight

The insight

Validation is not resolution.

A validation API checks one address in isolation. It will never tell you that six rows in your CRM are one physical house — the question behind delivery consolidation, lending fraud rings, and duplicate patient records.

We found the failure in production banking data

In the IFSC dataset, MICR codes are meant to identify one physical branch. Of 18 record pairs sharing a MICR, only 8 were actually the same building. India's banking system itself cannot keep one building as one record.

And the fix is a language problem

The variance is Hinglish, Devanagari, transliteration drift — HANUMANNAGAR vs HANUMAN NAGAR. It fails at the language layer, not the formatting layer. That is why it stayed unsolved, and why Indic-native models finally make it solvable.

Same door, or different door? Nobody answers that question for India.

How it works

One pipeline, seven steps.

ParseL0

sarvam-105b extracts structure from any script — landmarks kept as first-class fields with spatial relations, because in India the landmark is the address.

ResolveL1

Coarse signals gate the neighbourhood; fine signals decide the door. Locality agreement spans tens of thousands of households — it is never proof of a match.

ScoreL2

Call-risk before dispatch, with reasons and the single field whose answer most reduces risk. Rule-based on purpose — ops teams can't action a black box.

DIGIPIN cell assignment (India Post's official grid — encode, decode, group are live) operates on coordinates; free text reaches it once a geocoder is in the loop.

lattice08 / Why now

Why now

Two forces just crossed.

01 · The state built the destination

DIGIPIN is live.

India Post + ISRO shipped DIGIPIN — a geo-code for every 4m×4m cell (May 2025) — and DHRUVA, an "Address-as-a-Service" DPI framework, now follows it (framework stage, 2026). The state has committed: addresses are infrastructure. But DIGIPIN converts coordinates, not text — and legacy CRMs hold hundreds of millions of free-text strings. The state builds rails, not products — UPI created PhonePe. The legacy bridge onto these rails is the product. Lattice is that bridge.

02 · The language layer became solvable

Indic-native models arrived.

This problem is decades old. It stayed unsolved because it fails at the language layer — Hinglish, Devanagari, transliteration drift — not the formatting layer. That is exactly what the Sarvam stack unlocked. This product could not be built in 2020.
